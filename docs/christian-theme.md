# A Christian theme for the short desk

Written 6 September 2026 against the plan in `pons-tech.md`: the short desk (Locate's contracts on
Morpho markets where the meme is the loan token and USDG the collateral) with BOW's fee
architecture, launched on Pons V2. The question was whether the coin can carry a Christian theme.
It can, and the fit is better than a coat of paint, because the desk's economics already contain
a tithe. The warning comes first, though, because the evidence is blunt.

Two lessons. A faith name on its own is worth nothing: it has been tried twenty times here and
priced at zero twenty times. And the only faith coin still above $1M anywhere is the one that had
a purpose people could point to. Nobody, anywhere, has put a faith theme on a working product.
On Pons, TA holds about $2M as a charity that publishes every fee, so this chain will pay for
visible giving. The theme has to sit on the desk, not replace it.

Recommendation: the coin is **TITHE**, the vault is **the Storehouse**, a short is **a sling**,
the payout is **Manna**. The desk's site can be plain about being a memecoin with a product.

## The objection, answered before anyone raises it

Every thread will say the same thing: the Bible forbids lending at interest. It is a fair point
and it has to be met on its own ground.

What Scripture says. Interest to the poor and to fellow Israelites is forbidden: Exodus 22:25,
Leviticus 25:35-37, Deuteronomy 23:19, Psalm 15:5, Nehemiah 5. Jesus says lend expecting nothing
back (Luke 6:34-35) and overturns the money-changers' tables (Matthew 21:12). And the same
Scripture permits interest on loans to the foreigner, meaning commercial loans (Deuteronomy
23:20), and in the parable of the talents the master rebukes the servant who buried the money:
"you ought to have invested my money with the bankers, and at my coming I should have received
what was my own with interest" (Matthew 25:27; Luke 19:23).

What the tradition did with it. Aquinas condemned usury outright. Calvin's 1545 letter on the
question allowed moderate interest on commercial loans and forbade it on loans to the poor, and
that settlement is the one nearly every modern Christian banks under.

Where the desk stands. It lends only to speculators who post USDG to short a memecoin; it never
lends to the needy and never will; it tithes its income; it writes off bad debt from Joseph's
Reserve rather than chasing anyone; and it prints the collateral verse on the borrow page instead
of hiding the risk. Say all of that on the site in three sentences. The argument will still
happen, and it is the best engagement the coin will get.

## Lines not to cross

- No image of Christ as a mascot, no JESUS ticker, no crucifix in the logo. The lore is drawn from
  the stories (David, Noah, Joseph, manna, the tithe, Jubilee), never from the person of Christ or
  the sacraments. No "baptize your bag", no "communion".
- No prosperity gospel. Never "buy and be blessed", never a verse as a price target, never
  Malachi's floodgates as an APY. The community will make that joke; the project does not.
- Do not market into churches or congregations. Bubblemaps found 63% of this chain's wallets
  losing money; the audience includes people who cannot afford to. The disclaimer says memecoin,
  says leverage, says unaudited, in plain words.
- The charity slice must be real and provable: an on-chain transfer every Jubilee to a recipient
  chosen by staker vote from a short list of relief organisations, with receipts. Engiven and
  The Giving Block take crypto for U.S. nonprofits and issue receipts, and Christianity Coin's
  $81K shows even a dead coin's giving was checked. If the slice is ever skipped, the theme is
  over.
- Be a memecoin with a product, not a ministry. The moment the team speaks for God, it is done.

## What changes against the BOW-shaped plan

Nothing in the contracts. The changes are names, one fee slice, and cadence:

- **The Tithe split**, replacing BOW's 35/30/25/10: Manna to stakers 30%, Joseph's Reserve 30%,
  the Refiner's fire 20%, treasury 10%, the tithe of the tithe 10%. The treasury takes less than
  BOW's because the coin's own Pons fees (base fee plus a 1% creator tax, paid in USDG) already
  fund it; split those fees half to treasury, half to Manna.
- **Sunday Service**: a signed weekly report posted on chain and on the site; no market listings
  or parameter changes on Sundays.
- **Jubilee**: every seventh Sunday, one transaction burns the accumulated Refiner's fire and
  sends the tithe of the tithe. The first Jubilee is a date the community can count down to.
- **Launch story**: the Storehouse live before the curve opens, with three Giants (PONS, CASHCAT,
  AI) showing a borrow rate; the fourth and fifth stones (Index, then BONER) when their pools'
  observation cardinality clears 1,800. The curve is the Wilderness; graduation is the Jordan;
  the first Sunday Service is the Sunday after.
- **Pairing**: USDG, so Manna and the Storehouse are the same asset; SPY if the narrative is worth
  a swap. Team allocation 2% to 3%, locked, as PARE did, and the team tithes its own allocation
  to the same recipient the stakers choose.

## Verdict

Yes. The desk is the only product on the chain whose numbers already contain the theme, and the
theme gives the desk what the winners in `pons-tech.md` all have and the treasury coins lacked: a
heartbeat with a vocabulary. The risk is not the market, which has never once paid for a faith
name here; it is taste, and the section above is the whole defence.
